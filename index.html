Abstract

Why EmoShift?

Most speech deepfake detection evaluations focus on utterances with relatively stable prosody, while real speech can change emotional state within an utterance. EmoShift investigates whether such changes alter the evidence that detectors rely on when distinguishing bona fide from synthetic speech.

We assemble a benchmark across four corpora with both stable-emotion (AA) and emotion-transition (AB) conditions. We compare detector responses on synthetic and bona fide speech and study how changes differ across corpora and detector architectures. The findings highlight that within-utterance affective dynamics are an important dimension of robustness evaluation.

This public description summarizes the 16,960-sample release subset (ESD, OMG-Emotion, IEMOCAP and MSP-Podcast). It does not claim that every sample is same-text paired.

Results

What changes under emotion transitions?

Missed synthetic speech

In in-the-wild material, within-utterance emotion transitions can increase missed spoofs at operating thresholds calibrated on stable-emotion speech. The reported maximum increase in the broader study is 25.3 percentage points.

False alarms on genuine speech

For conversational bona fide audio, transitions can also make genuine recordings more likely to be labeled as synthetic by some detectors.

Effects depend on dataset, detector and operating point. The full numerical tables will be published with the anonymous paper when an approved link is available.

Analysis

Interpreting detection behavior

Detector differences

Responses vary across detector families. Some SSL-based systems show pronounced changes under emotional transitions, while other architectures respond differently to arousal and recording context.

Controls and confounds

The evaluation distinguishes bona fide and synthetic changes, tests generation without splicing, and uses endpoint controls where available. Pairing assumptions are corpus-specific rather than universal.
