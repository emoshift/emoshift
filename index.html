EmoShift.

When speech changes emotion, do deepfake detectors change their minds?

A benchmark for evaluating the effect of within-utterance emotional transitions on synthetic speech detection across controlled, in-the-wild, and conversational audio.

01 / Motivation

Emotion is dynamic. Detection should account for it.

Speech deepfake detectors are commonly evaluated on emotionally stable recordings, but speakers naturally change their emotional state within an utterance. EmoShift studies how those transitions affect decisions on both bona fide and synthetic speech.

Research question

Can emotional dynamics mask synthetic speech?

Compare stable and transitioning synthetic utterances under consistent evaluation thresholds.

Complementary question

Do transitions affect genuine speech?

Measure changes in false alarms for bona fide recordings from naturalistic conversations.

02 / Benchmark

Four corpora, two conditions.

The release design contains 16,960 utterances: 7,240 bona fide and 9,720 synthetic samples. Audio access is subject to source-corpus permissions.

03 / Experimental design

Stable versus transitioning emotion.

Both conditions are represented in bona fide and synthetic speech, permitting complementary analyses of missed spoofs and false alarms.

AA
A → A

Stable emotion

An utterance or segment pair selected to maintain a similar emotional state.

AB
A → B

Emotion transition

An utterance with a change in emotional state within the recording.

Matching and controls differ across corpora. In particular, the same-text control is not assumed for every bona fide pair.

04 / Findings

Different conditions, different errors.

At operating thresholds calibrated on stable speech, emotion transitions can change detector behavior. The benchmark examines missed synthetic speech in in-the-wild audio and false alarms on genuine conversational speech.

Missed spoofs

Transitions in synthetic speech can lower spoof-detection sensitivity for some detector and corpus combinations.

Bona fide false alarms

Conversational emotional shifts can also increase incorrect spoof predictions on real recordings.
